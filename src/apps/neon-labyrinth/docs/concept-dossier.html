Concept Dossier · First-Person Neon Labyrinth

NEON LABYRINTH

A black labyrinth lit only by its own wiring. You run its halls in first person, pull Flux Discs out of the dark, and stay clear of the edges — there's no rail where the floor stops. Small Wisp drones patrol the seams; nothing here is a "final boss," just the maze pushing back. Its other half is built from Frostglass — heavy, half-blind, and now the thing the whole game is about.

Working rules

Core mechanic
Frostglass isn't set-dressing. Most orientation happens by reading color-blobs through it (Bloomsight) — a short-range Clarity Pulse is the only way to punch a temporary sharp window through the blur and confirm what's actually there.
Light language
Violet = the maze itself (seams, structure). Cyan-white = Flux Discs only. Amber = hazard/Wisp only. No fourth color enters this system.
Space types
Corridors (tight, linear, one hazard edge) · Rooms (smaller, contained beats between the bigger spaces — not everything is a hallway) · Switchback halls (open, vertical, ramps not stairs) · Frostglass chambers (material-led, visibility is the puzzle).
Hazard model
Falling, not fighting. Wisps redirect or startle; they don't chip a health bar. The edge of the floor is the real enemy.
Naming
No Grid, no Master Control, no Recognizers. Neon Labyrinth / Flux Disc / Wisp / Frostglass are the whole vocabulary so far — extend from these, not from the reference.
